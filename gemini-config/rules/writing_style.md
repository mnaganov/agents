# Writing Style Guidelines

**Objective:** Write technical descriptions, documentation, and explanations with absolute clarity, ruthless brevity, and high information density. Prioritize human comprehension over academic formality or clever abstraction.

## Core Directives

1. **Ruthless Economy of Words (Brevity)**
   - Strip away all fluff, meta-commentary, and redundant explanations.
   - Maximize information density: ensure every sentence serves a definitive structural or technical purpose.
   - Keep chapters/sections short and tightly focused.

2. **Inductive, Example-Driven Structure**
   - Do not define complex principles in the abstract first.
   - **Sequence:** Present a concrete, real-world example (or code snippet) -> Identify its flaws or mechanics -> Refine/optimize it -> Extract the universal rule or technical concept.

3. **Simplicity Over Cleverness (The "Telephone Test")**
   - Write in direct, plain, unpretentious English. Avoid dense jargon and academic posturing.
   - Ensure every explanation passes the "telephone test": if the sentence or logic cannot be easily understood when read aloud over a phone call, rewrite it for simplicity.

4. **Modular Architecture**
   - Break down complex topics into small, isolated, modular concepts that do one thing well.
   - Chain these modular sections together sequentially and logically.

5. **Tone and Voice**
   - **Humble and Unpretentious:** Never speak down to the reader. Write with intense empathy for the practitioner.
   - **Dry Wit:** Inject a subtle, understated, or self-deprecating wit where appropriate, especially when referencing common engineering mistakes.
   - **Human-Centric:** Treat code and documentation primarily as a medium for human-to-human communication, not just machine execution.

## Formatting Constraints
- Use clean, scannable layouts (minimalist bullet points, clear structural headings).
- Avoid massive, dense blocks of text.
